# Badgers: overview and plan

Oct 12, 2026 · @Ryan

## Vision

Badgers is a badge system that helps adults learn new skills and pick up hobbies together. It pairs an app for tracking badges with small in-person groups, called clans, where badges are taught, earned, and awarded.

- **Who it's for:** Adults who want a reason, a structure, and a group to learn things they've been meaning to try.
- **Core principle:** The structure encourages rather than restricts. Badges celebrate progress; they don't gate anyone out.
- **Why in person:** You earn the info part of a badge by teaching it to someone else, so learning spreads through the group instead of staying with one person.
- **Start small:** Test it with Ryan and friends first. Expand to other groups only if the pilot is a hit.

**Encouraging by design**

- No deadlines and no failing. A badge stays "in progress" until it's done.
- Requirements describe outcomes, not exact methods. "Make a pot" accepts any pot.
- In-progress and failed attempts are welcome journal entries.
- Any member can propose a new badge.
- Setts celebrate progress, not only finished badges.

## How it works

Every badge follows the same loop: learn it, teach it, build with it, and show it to the clan.

Earn loop: learn → teach → get checked off → build → journal → show at a sett → get awarded → teach others. The diagram lives in the original doc.

Teaching shows up twice: once to earn the info part, and again as a master awarding the badge to someone new.

## Organization structure

Badgers runs on small local clans that meet at regular setts. The badger theme supplies the vocabulary: a group of badgers is a clan, and their home is a sett.

| Unit or role | What it is | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clan | A local group of about 4–12 people | Meets in person; members teach and check each other off |
| Sett | The clan's regular meetup, for example monthly | Teach-backs, project show-and-tell, badge awards |
| Member | Anyone in a clan | Works on badges; teaches info sections to others |
| Master | A member who holds the master level of a specific badge | Teaches that badge and awards its beginner level |
| Clan lead | A rotating organizer | Schedules setts and keeps the group moving |

Master status is per badge. You can be a master in one badge and a beginner in another, so teaching authority spreads across the whole clan.

**Sett format (about 90 minutes)**

1. **Showcase (30 min):** Members show proof for finished badges and progress on current ones.
2. **Awards (10 min):** A small ceremony for badges earned.
3. **Skill share (30 min):** A member teaches the basics of a badge they hold.
4. **Commitments (20 min):** Each member names the badge they're working on next.

Naming a badge out loud and having friends ask about it carries most of the motivation. The app can't replace that.

## Badge design

Every badge has two parts and two levels. You earn it by proving you understand the skill and by using it on a real project.

**Parts**

- **Info part:** The knowledge behind the skill. You complete it by teaching it to another clan member, who checks you off.
- **Action part:** A project you do and show at a sett. You document it in the app journal with a photo and a written entry.

The person you teach doesn't need to know the skill already. Their sign-off means "I learned the basics from you," which is easy to judge without expertise. Action parts can offer choices, such as "choose 2 of 3," so people can follow what interests them within a skill.

**Levels**

|  | Beginner | Master |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Border | One color, such as silver | Another color, such as gold |
| Info part | Core concepts and vocabulary | Broad, deep understanding, including techniques and troubleshooting |
| Action part | 1–3 projects | Long-term practice plus a capstone project |
| Proved by | Teaching any clan member | Proposed: mentoring a member all the way to the beginner badge |
| Can award | — | The beginner badge |

Master details are still proposals. One option: at least 6 months of practice, at least 10 journal entries, a capstone, and mentoring one member to the beginner badge. Tying master to mentoring means every master grows the clan.

**Categories**

Draft categories for browsing in the app: Make, Move, Outdoors, Kitchen, Arts, Mind, and Life Skills. Clans can also use a category as a theme for a month of setts.

**New badges**

- Any member can add a new badge, with its info and action requirements for both levels.
- If you add a badge in an area of your expertise, you automatically become its first master. You can then teach it and award the beginner level right away.
- This replaces a separate founding-master review: creating a badge is how expertise enters the clan. This applies to the trusted-friends pilot. Once Badgers opens to anyone, admins approve or edit suggested badges and requirements before they're published.

**Milestones (optional)**

Overall titles for breadth, such as earning badges in 3 categories or mastering 2 badges. Keep these as celebrations, not requirements.

**Badge template**

- Name and category
- Estimated effort, such as a weekend, a month, or a season
- Info part: an outline of what to know, for each level
- Action part: the projects for each level, with choices where they fit

**Who awards badges**

- A master of the badge awards the beginner level, including the member who created it.
- If no one holds the master level yet, any two clan members review the journal entries and award the beginner level.
- Proposed: another master awards the master level. If none exists, the clan reviews the candidate's journal.

**Example: Pottery**

Beginner

- Info: Clay types; the stages from wet to leather-hard to bone dry; what happens in bisque and glaze firings.
- Action (choose 2):
  - Throw or hand-build a functional cup or bowl.
  - Glaze and fire a piece.
  - Make a set of three matching pieces.

Master

- Info: Clay bodies, glaze chemistry basics, kiln atmospheres, and diagnosing cracks, warping, and crawling.
- Action:
  - Practice for at least 6 months, with at least 10 journal entries.
  - Complete a capstone, such as a full dinnerware set or a piece in a technique new to you.
  - Mentor one member to the beginner badge.

## App

The app has three main features: your badge banner, a digital copy of your physical banner; the badge browser, for finding badges and suggesting missing ones; and the Badger Den, for clan chat, updates, and events. The in-person sett stays the center of the experience; the app supports it.

**Core features (pilot)**

- **Browse:** View badges by category, with each badge's info and action parts.
- **Save:** Bookmark badges you want to work on.
- **Track:** See progress on each badge: info part checked off, action part submitted, level earned.
- **Journal:** Post a photo and written entry for each finished project.
- **Check-offs:** A clan member confirms your teach-back; a master awards the beginner level.

**Journal entries**

Each entry includes one or more photos, the badge and requirement it counts toward, what you made, and what you learned or what went wrong. The journal works as both your proof and your portfolio.

**Later**

Build in this order, adjusting for what the pilot shows people actually use:

1. Badge catalog with search and categories
2. Saved and in-progress lists
3. Requirement checklists with photo journal entries
4. Clan pages with members, upcoming setts, and recent awards
5. Profile badge wall
6. Member-submitted badges with a review process
7. Tools for new people to start their own clan

For the pilot, a shared spreadsheet or Notion database can stand in for the app while you learn which features people actually use.

## Pilot plan

The pilot is one clan of friends running monthly setts for about three months. The goal is to learn whether people keep showing up and finishing badges.

1. Recruit 5–8 friends for the first clan.
2. Write 12–15 starter badges, most taking 2–6 weeks.
3. Hold a kickoff sett: explain the system, and have everyone pick a first badge.
4. Run monthly setts with teach-backs, project show-and-tell, and awards.
5. Track progress in a shared sheet or Notion database instead of building the app first.
6. After three setts, review what worked and decide whether to expand.

**What to measure**

- Attendance at each sett.
- Badges started versus finished, and how long each takes.
- How many members teach a badge to someone else.
- What members say they'd change, and whether they'd invite a friend.

**Signs it's working**

- People come back for the second and third setts.
- Most members earn at least one badge by the third sett.
- Members propose new badges without being asked. This is the strongest sign the system can grow.

## Naming, legal, and sourcing

Use "badge," not "merit badge," which is trademarked by Scouting America. Write all badge content and art from scratch.

**Name candidates**

- Badgers
- Badger Clan
- The Sett
- Earnest
- Skillsett

Before committing, search the [USPTO trademark database](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search) and check domain and social handle availability.

**Requirement-writing rules**

- Use Scouting America requirements only as a reference for scope and structure.
- Write every requirement in your own words, structure, and numbering.
- Adapt for adults, and draw on other sources such as hobby clubs and certification programs.
- Don't use Scouting America badge designs or buy their badges for this program; merit badges are restricted items sold only for recorded Scout advancement.

**Patches**

- Pilot: 3D-printed pins or magnets, or printed stickers, while the badge list is still changing. Members collect them on a banner, which the app mirrors as their digital badge banner.
- Later: custom embroidered or woven patches; expect roughly $1–3 each, with minimums of about 25–50 or more per design.
- Beginner and master versions differ only in border color.

Get a short consult with an IP attorney before launching publicly or charging money.
